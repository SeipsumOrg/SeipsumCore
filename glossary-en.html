Definitions, Misconceptions & Dynamics — Concept Glossary


This page exists last on purpose. A glossary whose concepts are not yet developed is a list of promises: definitions written ahead of the pages that earn them, each one a reference to something that does not exist yet. Built after the concepts have been developed, the same list becomes a hub — every definition already earned somewhere, every entry a door back into the page that built it.


The objective here is not to introduce new ideas, but to hold the site's established vocabulary in one place: what each concept means, what it is commonly mistaken for, and where its mechanics are developed. Each definition is a single paragraph. Each entry links to the page where the concept does its work.





🔊 Every page can be listened to — press “Listen” on any section, or “Listen to whole page”.



The Main Observation


A glossary is not a dictionary. A dictionary defines words by other words; a glossary defines operations by their mechanics — and mechanics cannot be compressed into a definition without loss. What a definition can do is mark the entrance: name the concept, correct its most common misreading, and point to where the structure is actually developed. The definition is the surface; the linked page is the building.

The Concepts


Field


The field is the totality of what a person holds and operates at any moment: content, structure, unresolved load, and the operations running on all of it. It is the site's unit of analysis — because cognition cannot be examined as a property of the person, only as the state of the field the person is operating. Commonly mistaken for "the mind" or "thinking in general"; the field is not a faculty but a state — structured or unstructured, differentiated or not, occupied or available.

Developed on: every page — see in particular Structured Thinking and Human Cognition & AI.


Differentiation


Differentiation is the operation that separates what a field holds: levels from each other, the load-bearing from the accompanying, what is known from what is assumed. It is the site's central operation because every other mechanic depends on it — performance, transfer, ignoring, and decision-making are all executed by the separations a field has made. Commonly mistaken for "analysis"; analysis works on content, differentiation works on structure — what something is is a different question from what level it sits on.

Developed on: every concept page — see in particular Cognitive Performance and Multitasking.


Load-bearing


The load-bearing is the small set of elements in a situation on which the outcome actually depends — separated from the many that merely accompany them. Effort placed on the load-bearing performs; effort placed on the accompanying produces volume. Commonly mistaken for "the important things"; importance is a judgement made without structure, while the load-bearing is identified by structure — it is discovered, not decided.

Developed on: Cognitive Performance, Value Conflicts and Hidden Trade-offs, Multitasking.


Levels


Levels are the distinct kinds of work a demand can ask for — decision, generation, verification, separation — and the distinct layers a field can hold content at. Confusing levels is the mechanic behind most wasted effort: effort applied at the visible level while the load-bearing sits at another. Commonly mistaken for "steps" or "priorities"; levels are not a sequence to follow but a structure to respect — each level is performed by a different operation.

Developed on: Cognitive Performance, On Complexity.


Completion


Completion is the general-cognition operation that finishes a pattern before it is examined: the field (or the machine) fills the gap, produces the missing piece, arrives at the answer — fluently, automatically, and without marking the output as a completion rather than a finding. Completions are not errors; they are outputs produced by process, not by differentiation of process — and the difference matters exactly where they are trusted as findings.

Developed on: Introduction to Human Cognition & AI Cognition, Signal vs. Noise in Thinking, Platform Dynamics.


General cognition


General cognition is the layer of shared operations beneath both human and machine processing: pattern, prediction, completion, association. It is what the two cognitions have in common — operations only, never nature, never ranking. Commonly mistaken for a claim that human and machine cognition are the same thing on different substrates; the shared layer makes the mechanics comparable, and it is precisely the layer that cannot examine itself.

Developed on: Introduction to Human Cognition & AI Cognition.


Metacognition


Metacognition is the human operation on the field's own processing: knowing what kind of process is producing what, what is load-bearing, what may be held and what must be verified. It belongs exclusively to human cognition — not because machines cannot inspect outputs, but because inspecting what kind of process produced what is not an operation the completion-producing layer performs on itself. It is the site's answer to every "how is this managed" question: the managing happens one level above the performing.

Developed on: Introduction to Human Cognition & AI Cognition, Cognitive Performance.


The unresolved


The unresolved is load carried without clarification: an open item, a deferred decision, a level not separated, a conflict not represented. It is the site's most load-bearing concept because it is the common cause beneath underperformance, disorientation, instability, and noise — none of which are produced by the difficult or the unknown, but by the unclarified. Its signature construction: what destabilises a field is not what it cannot know, but what it has not resolved.

Developed on: every page — the "the unresolved, not the unknown" construction closes each page's uncertainty section.


The intangible layer


The intangible layer is where the site's work happens: clarification, separation, identification, matching — operations that produce no visible artifact. What they stabilise is tangible: capacity released, work that resolves, fatigue that rest can remove. The relation is the site's economic claim: the intangible is not less real than the tangible; it is the layer where the tangible is produced.

Developed on: Cognitive Performance — and every page's "What This Means" section.


Signal vs. noise


Signal is what a demand actually needs from the field; noise is everything that arrives alongside it and competes for the same capacity. The distinction is not a property of the information but a relation: the same item is signal in one structure and noise in another — which is why noise cannot be defined generically, only separated, each time, by the field that knows its demand.

Developed on: Signal vs. Noise in Thinking, Knowing What to Ignore.


Ignoring


Ignoring is not the absence of attention but an operation: the deliberate non-processing of what has been examined and found not load-bearing. It requires structure first — what cannot be separated cannot be safely ignored, only indiscriminately filtered. Commonly mistaken for neglect or for filtering; the undifferentiated field cannot ignore at all, because it cannot distinguish what it is ignoring from what it is keeping.

Developed on: Knowing What to Ignore.


Complexity


Complexity is not the count of elements but the count of unmanaged relations among them: a situation with many components and separated levels is not complex to a differentiated field, and a situation with few components and mixed levels is. Complexity is therefore a relation between a demand and a field's structure — not a property of the situation alone.

Developed on: On Complexity.


Value conflict


A value conflict is a trade-off in which both sides are held: nothing is being weighed against nothing — something valued is being given up, whatever is chosen. Its mechanic is representation: the conflict that is not represented is still enacted, blindly. Commonly mistaken for indecision; the conflict is not the hesitation but the structure beneath it, and it resolves not by speed but by representation and pricing.

Developed on: Value Conflicts and Hidden Trade-offs.


Cognitive performance


Cognitive performance is the output of a field under demand: not speed, not volume, not effort — but the quality of the match between the field's organisation and what the situation requires. The same capacity, differently organised, performs differently. Commonly mistaken for a property of the person; it is a property of the relation, which is why it cannot be increased directly — only reorganised.

Developed on: Cognitive Performance.


Multitasking


Multitasking is not doing several things at once but switching between them — serial attention presented as parallel, with each switch paying a release and a re-assembly that produce nothing toward either task. Its cost is invisible because each payment is small; its trade is priced in depth. Commonly mistaken for a skill; what practice improves is the switching, and faster switching makes the trade more attractive, not less expensive.

Developed on: Multitasking.


Depth


Depth is the field's hold on a task below its surface: the levels beneath the visible work, where the load-bearing is kept. Depth is not resumable; it is rebuilt — which is why it is the quantity multitasking trades away and the condition coherent work protects. Commonly mistaken for slowness or focus-as-a-feeling; depth is a structural condition, reachable only when switching is suspended for a duration the task defines.

Developed on: Multitasking, Cognitive Performance.


Communication


Communication is the attempted transfer of structure from one field to another — across a gap that never closes, since the words carry but the levels, load-bearing and separations that gave them meaning do not travel with them. What arrives is always a reconstruction, built by the receiving field from its own materials. Commonly mistaken for the delivery of a message; delivery is arrival, and nothing in the arrival guarantees the building.

Developed on: Communication Dynamics.


Reconstruction


Reconstruction is what the receiving field builds from what arrives — assembled from its own levels, its own load-bearing, its own unresolved. It is not a copy and cannot be one, because the gap the transfer crosses does not carry structure. The mechanic of most communication failure is not a bad reconstruction but an unexamined one: two fluent vocabularies meeting, two different buildings behind them.

Developed on: Communication Dynamics.


Platform


A platform is not a tool but an environment of pre-built structure: surfaces, defaults, and completions supplied at scale, structured by its builders for their purposes. It is not neutral and not hostile — it is structured, and a field that enters without differentiating receives the structure as surroundings, unexamined. Defaults act as defaults regardless of intent; surroundings are what a field examines last.

Developed on: Platform Dynamics.


Defaults


Defaults are what an environment supplies without being asked: the feed, the format, the ranking, the speed — structure that does not present itself as a choice and is therefore not examined as one. The mechanic of adoption is exposure, not decision; the counter-mechanic is surfacing: a default that has been named can be adopted as a decision, and an inherited default is a decision that was never made.

Developed on: Platform Dynamics.


Uncertainty


Uncertainty is the condition of incomplete knowledge — and, on this site, the recurring test of every mechanic: under uncertainty, the field verifies less and assumes more, and every page's question becomes whether the assumption is visible. Uncertainty does not remove the possibility of coherence; it changes what coherence can stand on: not fewer assumptions, but visibility on which assumptions the field's structure stands.

Developed on: every page's uncertainty section — see in particular Cognitive Performance, Communication Dynamics, Platform Dynamics.

What This Means


The glossary adds no concepts. Every definition above compresses a structure that exists in full on a linked page — and compression loses, which is the point: the definition exists to correct the common misreading and mark the entrance, not to replace the development. A visitor who reads only this page has read the site's vocabulary; a visitor who follows one link has begun reading the site.


The result is not a summary of the site. It is a map of it — accurate because the territory was drawn first.
