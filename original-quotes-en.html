Original Quotes — Lines, and the Structures Behind Them

Every quote below was written on one of the pages of this site — and each one links back to the page it was compressed from.

The Main Observation

A quote is a compression — and compression loses. Each line below stands on a structure it cannot carry with it: levels, separations, load-bearing variables that were developed across an entire page. Read alone, a quote is a surface. Followed to its source, it is an entrance. That is why every quote on this page is a link: the line is the doorway, and the page behind it is the building.

The quotes are grouped the way the site's concepts are grouped — the field, the intangible and the tangible, signal and noise, situations, work, transfer between fields, and the examining itself — because a line about the field reads differently once the lines about work have been read. The order is part of the page.

What This Means

The result is not a collection of quotable lines. It is a map of entrances. Each quote compresses a page; each link decompresses it. A visitor who reads only the quotes has read the site at its surface — compressed, coherent, and incomplete by design. A visitor who follows one link has entered the building the line was cut from.

And the page performs its own mechanic: a quote is what arrives; the page behind it is what was load-bearing. The gap between the two is the site's whole subject — visible here in miniature, on every line.
